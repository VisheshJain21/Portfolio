The Brief // integrity of evidence

What was actually observed — and what wasn't.

Every claim in this dossier is tagged. This matters: a design decision built on a wrong assumption compounds. Where the sandbox network throttled a heavy WebGL payload before it finished, I say so rather than dressing a guess as a fact.

Inspection was performed in a real Chromium browser: reading the live DOM tree, computed styles, loaded font tables, network requests, and the global JavaScript namespace of each site, plus manual interaction (clicking, hovering, waiting out preloaders) and screenshots at multiple load states.

Confirmed means

  • Read directly from the page: a computed font-family, a network request, a framework global, a rendered pixel I saw.

Inferred means

  • A reasoned deduction from evidence — e.g. "custom WebGL engine" because window.THREE was absent — but not proven from source.

The preloader — a signature, not a spinner

Black screen. A thin centered progress bar (white fill on a dark track). And the memorable move: an oversized numeric counter cropped hard against the bottom-left corner, its digits rolling upward per-column like a mechanical odometer. Because the type is clipped by the viewport edge, only the top ~60% of each digit shows — a deliberate tension between "huge" and "hidden." Crucially, it is gated on real asset load, not a fixed timer: I watched it crawl as the WebGL payload downloaded.

Scroll narrative & sections

The document reads as a story, confirmed from the DOM tree: an intro thesis ("We create 3D visual storytelling…") → "Bold Ideas, Brought to Life" → an approach statement → a reel with a Watch button → Featured Work, ten projects each stamped with a discipline tag-list (concept · web · design · development · 3d · animation) → a manifesto "Where Creative Ideas Become Immersive Experiences" → the line "Step into a new world and let your imagination run wild," split into individual word spans (proof of word-by-word reveal choreography) → a CTA "Is Your Big Idea Ready to Go Wild?" → a footer with address, socials, newsletter, and an R&D "Labs" link.

Worth adapting

  • Asset-gated preloader with an oversized, cropped counter.
  • Word/line-split text reveals on scroll.
  • Project rows stamped with discipline tags.
  • One electric accent over a restrained base.
  • Mono for labels = quiet technical authority.

Do not copy

  • The ultramarine + Aeonik identity itself.
  • A custom WebGL engine — use Three/R3F.
  • Their exact "Bold Ideas" copy or layout.
Difficulty · adapt path

Preloader & split-text: easy–medium (GSAP + a splitting util). Hybrid DOM+WebGL architecture: medium and the right target for you. The bespoke renderer is the only hard part — and you should sidestep it with React Three Fiber.

The gateway preloader

It begins as a sphere textured from a field of ASCII glyphs — slashes and the digits 5 and 7 — with a teal "/75" count. As assets arrive it comes alive: electric-blue magnetic field lines radiate outward like iron filings around a dipole, twin "lens" poles form at the sides, and a ">>>" prompt invites a click. It is not passive — clicking perturbs the field. Entering the site plays a transition wipe. This is a threshold ritual: you don't load the site, you cross into it.

The hero, once inside

A floating pill-shaped navigation (WORK · lens-logo · CONTACT) with a soft glowing underside; "SCROLL DOWN" in teal mono; the "a" monogram inside a glowing ring trailing a 3D ribbon of light bent into an infinity loop; and a numbered project carousel (<< · 5. Magiksolo — Quantum World · >>). A "Toggle Audio" control confirms the spatial-sound layer.

Worth adapting

  • One signature interactive object as the hero's thesis.
  • The magnetic / field-line reaction to the cursor.
  • A floating pill nav with a soft glow.
  • An "enter" gesture as a moment of ceremony.
  • Numbered, navigable project carousel.

Do not copy

  • Rendering the whole UI in WebGL — kills a11y & SEO.
  • Building a bespoke engine.
  • The orb / dipole motif itself.
  • Mandatory audio or a hard gate on entry.
Difficulty · adapt path

A cursor-reactive hero object in Three.js/R3F with a custom shader: medium–hard but very achievable and high-impact. The all-WebGL architecture: very hard and, for a portfolio, the wrong trade. Take the feeling of the gateway; keep your content in the DOM.

Synthesis // the shared DNA

Three studios, one grammar.

Strip away the surface and the same nine principles recur. This is the vocabulary of "award-winning" — not effects, but discipline.

  • The preloader is a signature. Never a generic spinner; always bespoke, numeric/technical, and gated on real load — the first frame of the brand.
  • Restraint, then one voltage. A dark or desaturated, disciplined base carrying a single electric accent. Boldness is spent in exactly one place.
  • Serif or grotesque display + a monospace foil. The mono on labels and counters is what reads as engineering credibility.
  • WebGL as centerpiece, not wallpaper. 3D appears where it means something; the rest breathes.
  • Virtualized smooth scroll as the transport layer for choreography.
  • Type split to the word or character for reveals.
  • Immense negative space & oversized, sometimes cropped, numerals.
  • Signal words as content — "SCROLL DOWN", ">>>", "CONTINUE" — the interface narrates itself.
  • A minimal, floating navigation that stays out of the way.

Proposed Direction // for approval

They speak as "we." Your edge is that you speak as "I."

The studios sell a collective. A portfolio is intimate — one mind, one point of view. The distinctive move isn't more spectacle than a studio (you'll lose that arms race); it's a single, precise, personal instrument that a studio's committee aesthetic can't produce. Below is a starting proposal, offered for your direction — not a finished decision.

Working concept — "Instrument"

Your work presented like readouts on a calm, precise console: a dark, engineered space where one warm signal marks what matters. It nods to the shared grammar (bespoke preloader, mono labels, one accent, a WebGL centerpiece) but commits to warmth and intimacy where the references stay cold and corporate — a deliberate counter-position.

Motion language

  • Slow in, fast settle. An expressive ease (≈ cubic-bezier(.16,1,.3,1)), 0.6–1.2s, generous stagger. Nothing bouncy or "fun" — this is precision, not play.
  • Choreographed load over scattered effects: one orchestrated page-in sequence, then calm.
  • Reveal by masking — lines and words rise from clipped containers; images uncover, they don't fade.
  • Reaction, not decoration — magnetic buttons, a cursor the hero object acknowledges, parallax tied to real depth.

Interaction philosophy

Every section earns one intentional motion or interactive characteristic — no more. The site should feel like an instrument that responds precisely to touch, never a fairground of competing animations. Feedback is immediate and physical; ambient motion is near-zero.

Architecture & Strategy // how it's built

The pragmatic middle path.

Lusion's hybrid, not Active Theory's all-WebGL. Accessible DOM for all content; 3D layered in at the hero and one or two accent moments. This is the only architecture that is simultaneously award-caliber, crawlable, and maintainable by one person.

Recommended stack

Framework
Next.js (App Router) + TypeScript — SSR/crawlable content, image optimization, routing for project pages.
3D
React Three Fiber + drei + custom GLSL — the R3F ecosystem instead of a bespoke engine.
Motion
GSAP + ScrollTrigger for timelines; a splitting utility for text; Lenis for smooth scroll.
Styling
Modern CSS with custom-property tokens (fluid clamp() scale) — no heavy UI kit.
Ask-before-adding
Every dependency must beat native CSS / Web Animations / GSAP-you-already-have.

Responsive strategy — art-direct, don't shrink

  • Test 320 / 375 / 390 / 430 / 768 / 1024 / 1280 / 1440 / 1920 & ultrawide. Each breakpoint is composed, not scaled.
  • Touch replaces cursor interactions; magnetic/hover effects degrade to tap states.
  • WebGL adapts, never simply hides: fewer particles, simpler shader, adjusted camera, capped DPR on mobile.
  • Fluid type via clamp(); svh/dvh for mobile viewport truth; guard against horizontal overflow everywhere.

Performance budget

  • Stable 60fps target; correct single requestAnimationFrame loop; no layout thrash.
  • Lazy-load the 3D scene & dynamic-import heavy modules; defer non-critical assets.
  • Dispose Three.js geometries / materials / textures on unmount — no leaks.
  • Watch LCP, INP, CLS; cap devicePixelRatio; compress textures; modern image formats & responsive sizes.

Accessibility

  • Semantic HTML, logical heading order, meaningful alt text, keyboard paths, visible focus.
  • prefers-reduced-motion: strip large movement & parallax, hold content fully reachable — the WebGL falls back to a still frame.
  • A no-WebGL fallback (a poster image / gradient) so weak GPUs still get a complete site.

Implementation Roadmap // when we build

Order of operations.

Deliberately sequenced so nothing is built on an unproven foundation. Each phase is shippable and reviewable before the next begins.

P0

Direction & content lock

You approve (or redirect) this concept; we gather real project content, copy, and personality. No code until the subject is pinned.

P1

Foundation

Next.js + TS, token system & fluid type scale, inlined fonts, Lenis, layout shell, accessibility & reduced-motion baseline.

P2

Load choreography

Preloader + orchestrated page-in sequence + custom cursor + magnetic primitive.

P3

Structure

All sections built in DOM with scroll reveals — fully usable before any 3D exists.

P4

WebGL centerpiece

The hero instrument in R3F + GLSL, with its still-frame and no-GL fallbacks.

P5

Responsive art-direction

Compose every breakpoint; touch behaviors; overflow & viewport-unit pass.

P6

Performance

Profile LCP/INP/CLS & frame time; lazy-load; dispose 3D resources; cap DPR; optimize media.

P7

Audit & polish

Accessibility & reduced-motion pass, cross-browser, console/leak check, and a final visual-polish sweep.
